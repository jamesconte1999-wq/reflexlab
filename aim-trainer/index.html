Aim Trainer

Thirty targets, one at a time. Hit them fast, keep your accuracy clean. Your score is average time per target.

How the aim trainer works

A target appears at a random position in the arena. Click it and the next one appears instantly, thirty times in a row. We measure the time from each spawn to your hit and average it. Any click that lands off-target counts against your accuracy, so flicking wildly will cost you.

Using this as an FPS warmup

Two or three runs before a session of Valorant, CS2, Fortnite or Apex is a quick, free way to wake up your wrist and forearm. Aim for consistency across runs rather than one lucky fast time - a tight spread of scores transfers to games better than a single outlier.

Does aim training actually help in FPS games?

Yes. Target-switching drills build the same micro-corrections you use in shooters. Five minutes of warmup before playing measurably improves first-session accuracy for most players.

What is a good time per target?

Around 500 ms is typical for casual players. Regular FPS players land 300-400 ms, and dedicated aimers go below 280 ms while keeping accuracy above 95%.

Should I prioritize speed or accuracy?

Accuracy first. Speed follows naturally once your clicks are clean. Chasing speed with sloppy accuracy trains bad habits that are hard to unlearn.
