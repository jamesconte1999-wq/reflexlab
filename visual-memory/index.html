Visual Memory Test

Tiles flash for a moment, then hide. Click every tile you saw. The grid grows as you climb - three lives.

How the visual memory test works

Each level briefly reveals a set of highlighted tiles on a grid, then hides them. Click all of the positions you saw. Three wrong clicks within a level costs one of your three lives and replays the level with a new pattern. Clear a level and the next adds one more tile - and eventually a bigger grid.

See shapes, not squares

Your visual system is built for patterns, not coordinates. The reliable trick is to let the flash form a single image - "a hockey stick", "a smiley missing one eye" - and then click the image from memory. Players who name the shape consistently outlast players who try to memorize each square.

What is a good visual memory score?

Most players top out around level 9. Level 13-14 is roughly top 10%, and past 17 you are in rare territory. Fatigue hits this test hard, so scores swing day to day.

How can I remember more tiles?

Relax your gaze, take in the whole grid at once, and remember the overall shape the tiles make. Gestalt patterns hold far more information than separate positions.

Is visual memory trainable?

Yes. Visuospatial working memory shows consistent gains with regular practice, and improvements transfer to things like map reading and remembering layouts.
